## 28 primary sources mapped to opportunities

Dates identify initial publication or the inspected revision where stated. Several 2026 entries are recent preprints; method ideas are stronger grounds for experiments than their cross-paper headline scores. The opportunity IDs refer to this report and are our proposed applications, not capabilities demonstrated in Tessera.

### Evidence selection, changing state and evaluation

| Paper | What it teaches | Apply to | Limitation that changes the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P01. [Reproducing LightMem: Naive RAG Is Just as Good for Memory Management](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29104)** — July 2026 | With the constructed store fixed, changing the retriever moved answer accuracy from 58.1% to 75.5%. Raw-turn retrieval often won at equal retrieval depth; construction lost some answer-relevant information. | 04, 07, 15, 40; X1 | LightMem benefited under tight answering-token budgets. This is a reproduction of particular settings, not evidence that all structured memory is unnecessary. |
| **P02. [STALE: Can LLM Agents Know When Their Memories Are No Longer Valid?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06527)** — May 2026 | Tests state resolution, stale-premise resistance and behavioral adaptation. Its best tested model reached 55.2% overall; later events can make earlier assumptions unusable without explicitly contradicting them. | 01, 06, 10, 16; X3 | Implicit invalidation can itself be wrong. Treat newly unsupported claims as requiring revalidation, with traceable and reversible transitions. |
| **P03. [Beyond Memory Majority: Latent-Source Reasoning for Multi-Agent Memory Arbitration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19701)** — August 2026 | CAMA groups correlated evidence and retrieves missing independent support. Several agents can repeat one origin and create a false majority. | 03, 10, 24, 33; X2 | Experiments use correlation-aware benchmark variants with controlled paraphrases/derivations. Dependence is query-specific: one source can contain multiple complementary facts. |
| **P04. [When Continual Learning Moves to Memory: A Study of Experience Reuse in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.27003)** — April 2026 | Old experiences may remain stored while new near-duplicates make them hard to retrieve. Finer granularity and more frequent retrieval can degrade reuse. | 14, 15, 38, 39; X5 | Small controlled ALFWorld/BabyAI task-pair study, sparse retriever and few runs. It identifies a failure mechanism, not a verdict against atomic memories. |
| **P05. [UtilMem: Benchmarking Evidence Utilization in Long-Term Conversational Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30508v1)** — August 2026 | Tests integration of scattered evidence, implicit relevance and distractor resistance across 1,717 long-form cases. Useful memory requires applying the retrieved information. | 07, 37, 39, 40 | Uses judge-relative comparison to an oracle-context answer. Normalized robustness is not ordinary QA accuracy; human checks remain useful. |
| **P06. [Controlled Memory Interference in Continual LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.07622v1)** — August 2026 | Separates repetition and same-slot conflict from unrelated noise. Retrieval success and adoption of the correct update can fail independently. | 03, 06, 14, 38, 39; X5 | Controlled interventions are diagnostic and need reproduction on Tessera workloads. Do not generalize a reported condition into a population-wide failure rate. |
| **P07. [Sufficient Context: A New Lens on Retrieval Augmented Generation Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06037)** — November 2024; revised April 2025 | Relevant context can still be insufficient. Selective response needs evidence-completeness signals in addition to model confidence. | 08, 09, 40, 41 | RAG study rather than a memory-specific benchmark. Better correctness among answered queries may come at lower coverage; report the trade-off. |
| **P08. [QUMem: Personalized Memory for Query-Conditioned User-State Inference in LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16168)** — August 2026 | Episode construction, factual/preference/insight decomposition and task-conditioned retrieval support a current user-state view. | 02, 04, 06, 08, 25 | Already central to Tessera and mapped by #136–#146. Do not duplicate its gaps or require the paper's entire sequential agent pipeline. |
| **P09. [Zep: A Temporal Knowledge Graph Architecture for Agent Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13956v1)** — January 2025 | Links original episodes with derived facts and separates event validity from ingestion time. Supports the distinction between historical truth and historical knowledge. | 01, 06, 16, 28 | Provider-authored system results do not isolate bitemporality. New ingestion is not inherently more authoritative than earlier evidence. |
| **P10. [MemoTime: Memory-Augmented Temporal Knowledge Graph Enhanced Large Language Model Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13614)** — October 2025; revised February 2026 | Decomposes compound time questions into temporal operators and selects compatible evidence paths. | 06, 08, 10 | Structured temporal-KG QA differs from noisy source files and conversations. Start with deterministic interval fixtures before assisted planning. |
| **P11. [LongMemEval: Benchmarking Chat Assistants on Long-Term Interactive Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10813)** — October 2024; ICLR 2025 | Separates extraction, multi-session reasoning, updates, temporal reasoning and abstention across 500 questions; distinguishes indexing, retrieval and reading. | 07, 09, 37, 40, 41 | Already tracked in Tessera. Conversation QA alone does not establish improved autonomous execution; avoid repeated architecture selection on the same development subset. |
| **P12. [LongMemEval-V2: Evaluating Long-Term Agent Memory Toward Experienced Colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12493)** — May 2026 | Expands evaluation toward static/dynamic state, workflows, environment gotchas and premise awareness across 451 curated questions. | 19, 26, 37, 38 | Existing #106 already owns an adapter. These are questions over experience, not direct measurements of successful real-world task execution. |
| **P13. [GroupMemBench: Benchmarking LLM Agent Memory in Multi-Party Conversations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14498)** — May 2026 | Speaker identity, audience vocabulary and reply structure matter; simple BM25 competes strongly when complex ingestion loses these features. | 05, 07, 11, 25 | Synthetic English workplace conversations. Multilingual and multimodal behavior remain extensions, not findings established by the benchmark. |
| **P14. [Are We Ready For An Agent-Native Memory System?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24775)** — June 2026 | Studies representation/storage, extraction, retrieval/routing and maintenance across 12 systems and two baselines. No architecture dominates all workloads; localized maintenance can reduce cost. | 07, 17, 28, 32, 37, 40 | Results depend on workload and configuration. Use its decomposition to diagnose Tessera before adopting a new store or tree. |

### Learning, memory management and sharing

| Paper | What it teaches | Apply to | Limitation that changes the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P15. [CONTRAMEM: Learning Self-Evolving Procedural Memory from Contrasting Multi-Model Trajectories](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22533)** — August 2026 | Contrasts attempts on the same task and distills scoped function/task guidance through localized edits. Shared contrastive memory reaches 55.3% versus 47.2% for the paper's single-source pipeline. | 18, 19, 21, 26; X4 | The diversity increment is about 8.1 percentage points, not the full gain from no memory. Timing-related failures remain difficult; adapted controls are not original-method replications. |
| **P16. [From Memory to Skills: Evidence-Grounded Co-Evolution Governance for Long-Horizon LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16621)** — July–August 2026 manuscript | Separates traces, procedures and environmental knowledge; promotes supported procedures to callable skills with applicability and verification metadata. | 02, 18–22; X4 | Reliability and gain estimates are heuristic rather than causal. LLM transformations add cost/noise. Skill execution belongs to the host, not Tessera's deterministic core. |
| **P17. [Memᵖ: Exploring Agent Procedural Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06433v4)** — August 2025; revised April 2026 | Tests trajectory/procedure representations and transfer. Proceduralization improves some task outcomes and steps, but can worsen constraints. | 18, 19, 20, 26; X4 | In one GPT-4o TravelPlanner comparison, the hard-constraint score drops from 12.88 to 9.76. Check constraint compliance separately from average success. |
| **P18. [MemRL: Self-Evolving Agents via Runtime Reinforcement Learning on Episodic Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03192v2)** — January 2026; revised February | Combines relevance screening and utility learned from environmental outcomes without changing backbone weights. | 08, 14, 21, 26 | Utility feedback already belongs to #21. Multiple used memories complicate attribution; runtime repeated-epoch results and held-out transfer are different metrics. |
| **P19. [MemSkill: Learning and Evolving Memory Skills for Self-Evolving Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02474)** — February 2026 | Learns routines for constructing/managing memory and improves them using hard cases. This is learning how to write memory, not simply remembering task skills. | 04, 15, 22, 41 | Training and outcome supervision are required. Its LoCoMo split excludes adversarial questions and LongMemEval transfer uses approximately 100 examples; start offline with held-out validation. |
| **P20. [Memory as a Controlled Process: Learned Adaptive Memory Management for LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.13591)** — July 2026 | MemCon selects operations such as retrieval depth, retry, consolidation or no operation using a lightweight controller. | 08, 15, 17, 21 | A lightweight controller does not make the whole backend free of LLM calls. Unsupported maintenance actions can become no-ops. Begin with read-only policy selection. |
| **P21. [DeltaMem: Incremental Experience Memory for LLM Agents via Residual Trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03083)** — June 2026 | Stores reusable base procedures with environment-specific variations; reconstructs applicable chains rather than merging all instructions. | 15, 19, 26 | Extraction costs and benchmark-tuned thresholds matter; stale environmental knowledge remains an admitted gap. The residual tree is optional, applicability semantics are the first experiment. |
| **P22. [Multi-Agent Transactive Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19911)** — June 2026 | Diverse producers contribute trajectories that other agents can retrieve; transfer can improve execution without joint training. | 18, 24, 26 | Gains vary: simple shared retrieval improves WebArena only from 18.18% to 20.45% in the paper. Cross-benchmark reranker generalization is untested. |
| **P23. [Collaborative Memory: Multi-User Memory Sharing in LLM Agents with Dynamic Access Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.18279)** — May 2025 | Private/shared fragments and contributor/resource provenance support permission-aware reuse and changing access. | 23, 24, 34, 36 | Controlled moderate-scale simulations, not a demonstrated enterprise permission boundary. Implement enforcement deterministically, including derived records and revocation. |
| **P24. [LightMem: Lightweight and Efficient Memory-Augmented Generation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.18866)** — October 2025; revised February 2026 | Topic grouping, compression and offline consolidation reduce foreground context/maintenance work. | 15, 17, 32 | Distinguish total build-plus-query cost from online-only savings; use P01's reproduction to test whether compression preserves enough useful evidence. |
| **P25. [MemForest: An Efficient Agent Memory System with Hierarchical Temporal Indexing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23986)** — May 2026; revised July | Parallel extraction and localized dirty-path refresh make write-to-queryable latency a useful design target. | 17, 28, 32, 38 | Structural insertion depth is not total ingestion complexity. Benchmark gains do not prove a hierarchical tree is the right backend for Tessera. |

### Poisoning and trust boundaries

| Paper | What it teaches | Apply to | Limitation that changes the decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P26. [A Practical Memory Injection Attack against LLM Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.03704)** — March 2025 | MINJA shows ordinary interactions can seed persistent malicious memories that later influence behavior. | 02, 33, 35 | Attack success depends on task, retrieval and store population; this is not a measured vulnerability report about Tessera. Test benign utility beside attack resistance. |
| **P27. [Memory Poisoning Attack and Defense on Memory Based LLM-Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05504)** — January 2026 | Re-examines poisoning under different legitimate-memory populations and defense thresholds. Blocking suspicious writes has a usability trade-off. | 33, 35, 41 | Narrow EHR/model setting. Neither content moderation nor heuristic trust establishes a complete defense; avoid copying success percentages into product claims. |
| **P28. [When Does Belief-Based Agent Memory Help? Reliability-Conditional Updating and Provenance-Capped Poisoning Defense](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22030)** — June 2026; revised July | Belief updating is useful only under relevant uncertainty/conflict conditions. Content-asserted confidence is gameable; provenance caps also carry costs. | 02, 03, 09, 33 | Synthetic contradiction settings and unmatched headline baselines limit conclusions. Correct low-trust updates can be suppressed; complete taint propagation is characterized rather than implemented. |

### Recommended reading order

1. **P01 — LightMem reproduction:** challenge assumptions about the need for constructed memory and isolate retrieval versus representation effects.
2. **P02 — STALE:** define the next temporal failure cases beyond explicit newest-versus-oldest conflicts.
3. **P03 — Beyond Memory Majority:** extend source provenance into independent-evidence accounting.
4. **P04 — When Continual Learning Moves to Memory:** test whether growth degrades access to old abilities.
5. **P15 — CONTRAMEM:** turn #21 into a sharper contrastive learning experiment.
6. **P16 — From Memory to Skills:** design evidence-bearing promotion and revocation, if procedure reuse proves useful.
7. **P05 — UtilMem:** evaluate whether the selected evidence is actually used well.
8. **P14 — Agent-Native Memory System:** review operational trade-offs before choosing additional infrastructure.

QUMem and LongMemEval remain foundational reading, but these eight add distinct questions to the research already visible in Tessera. LiveMem, GraphMemix, A-MEM, FinPerMA, MemORAI, MemToC and RENDER are also already acknowledged in the repository; this expansion does not replace those research links or claim their planned capabilities are delivered.


## Supplementary paper inspected: limited-evidence watchlist

**[FSFM: A Biologically-Inspired Framework for Selective Forgetting of Agent Memory](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20300)** — April 2026.

This additional paper was inspected during research but is outside the 28 main-paper crosswalk. Its useful distinction is between decay, deletion and adaptive retention. Its telecom-domain evaluation and scale limits do not establish general reliability or complete security. Treat retention operations as different contracts and measure collateral forgetting. Do not repeat an absolute security-elimination claim as a guarantee.

**TESSERA interpretation:** opportunities 13–17 could evaluate retrieval demotion, archival and explicit deletion independently. Coordinate with #12/#19/#21/#73; a dedicated purge contract remains a candidate, not a newly opened feature issue. **Status:** DEFERRED research watchlist; no implementation or adoption.
